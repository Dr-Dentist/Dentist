Foods that secretly harm your teeth, and what to eat instead

April 6, 20264 min readDr. Asim Prakash, Araria

Your diet affects your teeth almost as much as your brushing does. It is not only about sugar — acidity, stickiness and how often you snack all matter.

The quiet offenders

  • Sweet tea and coffee, sipped slowly. Each sip restarts the acid attack. If you take sugar, finish the cup in one sitting rather than over an hour.
  • Cola and packaged fruit juice. Highly acidic and sugary — a double hit on the enamel. Even “diet” sodas are acidic.
  • Sticky sweets, toffees and dried fruit. They cling to the grooves of the teeth and keep feeding bacteria long after you have eaten.
  • Chips, crackers and white bread. Refined starch turns to sugar and packs into the biting surfaces.
  • Constant snacking. Ten small snacks means ten acid attacks. Three meals is far kinder to your teeth.
  • Chewing ice or using teeth as tools. A common cause of cracked teeth and broken fillings.

Better for your teeth

  • Water — especially after anything sweet or acidic. It rinses away sugar and acid.
  • Milk, curd, paneer and cheese. Calcium and protein help repair early enamel damage; cheese after a meal helps neutralise acid.
  • Crunchy vegetables and fruit like carrot, cucumber and apple. They stimulate saliva, which is the mouth's natural defence.
  • Nuts and seeds for a low-sugar snack.
  • Plain tea without sugar actually contains compounds that slow bacterial growth.
Timing tip: if you are going to have something sweet or acidic, have it with a meal rather than on its own between meals. Saliva flow is higher during meals and clears the acid faster. Wait about 30 minutes before brushing so you are not brushing softened enamel.

None of this means never enjoying a sweet. It means being deliberate about when and how often — and rinsing with water afterwards.
